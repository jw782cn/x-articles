### 创作过程：直觉、冲动与绞肉机 / Creative Process: Intuition, Impulse, and the Meat Grinder

**Bill:** When you're writing something like that, is it like every day you work on it, or a burst?
**Bill:** 你写这种东西的时候，是每天坐下来写，还是一阵灵感爆发？

**Ari:** It was kind of a burst. I don't have much of a process either. I think mostly it's an intuitive thing, but also an impulsive thing, where if something's there, I write it down and I tend to try to not censor myself or edit anything until it's down. And then once it's down, it becomes a process of just steeling yourself and not taking out the things that you know should probably not be there. That maybe need to be there for the integrity of the thing, but for your own peace of mind would be better taken out.
**Ari:** 算是爆发式的。我也没什么固定流程。基本上是直觉加冲动——如果有东西在那里，我就写下来，不审查不编辑，先全部倒出来。写完之后就是鼓起勇气的过程——不要删掉那些你觉得不该在里面的东西。那些东西可能为了作品完整性需要保留，但为了你自己的心理安宁最好删掉。

**Ari:** In this case I had been wanting to write something about the atmosphere, about just the environment we were living in. And that of course just includes the internet. Like what does it feel like to actually just be living in the internet? And then I realized that I had this script called Eddington, that I tried to get made eight years before I made Hereditary. And it didn't happen. It was just sort of like this contemporary western that I lost interest in. Because it needed something else. And then I realized, "Oh, I could take the basic structure and world of that script, and then use that as a thing to hang all these other ideas on." So that allowed me to write something in about three weeks.
**Ari:** 这次我一直想写点关于我们生活的氛围的东西——活在互联网里是什么感觉？然后我想起我有一个叫 Eddington 的剧本，是我拍《遗传厄运》八年前就试图拍的。没拍成。一个当代西部片，但我后来失去了兴趣，因为它缺少什么。然后我意识到"我可以用那个剧本的基本结构和世界观，把其他所有想法都挂上去。"所以大概三周就写完了。

**Ari:** And then I left it alone because Beau Is Afraid was the thing. And then in editing Beau, I started to polish it. And then I went to New Mexico, which is where I'm from, and drove around the state going to different small towns and pueblos, and interviewing sheriffs, police chiefs, public officials, mayors, and just started getting as full a picture as I could, of the state's politics. And once I met certain people that felt really great models for different characters, it started to get away from me in a good way.
**Ari:** 然后搁在一边，因为《博怕》才是当时要做的事。剪辑《博怕》的时候我开始打磨这个剧本。然后我去了新墨西哥——我老家——开车跑遍各个小镇和印第安保留地，采访警长、警察局长、政府官员、市长，尽可能全面地了解这个州的政治。遇到一些特别适合做角色原型的人之后，故事就开始脱离我的控制了——是好的那种。

**Bill:** Some people don't let that happen. You can see the movies people make where you're like, "Oh, they're playing with their army men." But I like that you don't do that.
**Bill:** 有些人不让这种事发生。你能看出那些电影里导演还在"摆弄小兵人"。但你不是这样。

### David Lynch 作为精神导师 / David Lynch as Spiritual Teacher

**Bill:** David Lynch, when he passed away, I watched all his movies again. And that was the thing that knocked me out — "God, these are so personal. These feel almost like embarrassingly personal."
**Bill:** David Lynch 去世的时候，我重看了他所有的电影。让我震惊的是——"天哪，这些太私人了。几乎是令人尴尬地私人。"

**Ari:** His vision is so complete and singular and idiosyncratic, and his picture of America. His death really hit me hard. In a way that kind of surprised me. I feel like part of that has to do with him being something of a spiritual teacher for a lot of people.
**Ari:** 他的视野如此完整、独特、异质，他对美国的描绘。他的死对我打击很大，出乎意料地大。我觉得部分原因是他对很多人来说是某种精神导师。

**Bill:** 我重看《橡皮头》时想"这是一个害怕拥有家庭、害怕承担责任、害怕有孩子的人。"但它是以这种方式表达出来的。

### 类型片作为盾牌 / Genre as a Shield

**Ari:** I think I use genre in some ways as a shield. It's a way of obfuscating. So in some ways it's cowardice, it's that I need to veil just how personal the stories are. I think if anything, I'm sometimes a little embarrassed by just how it verges on solipsism sometimes. But I know that just writing is hard and I can only ever really do it without effort if I'm in some kind of crisis or if I'm really stuck on something.
**Ari:** 我用类型片作为一种盾牌。它是一种遮掩。某种意义上这是怯懦——我需要面纱来遮挡故事到底有多私人。有时候我对它接近自恋的程度感到有点尴尬。但我知道写作很难，我只有在某种危机中或者真正被什么困住的时候才能毫不费力地写。

### Bill 被说服放弃的恐怖片 / Bill's Shelved Horror Script

**Bill:** I had written a horror movie right after Barry wrapped. And I had a meeting with a big producer who went, "This is so mean spirited and horrible." I was like, "Yeah, it's a horror movie." I think it was just because I was very vulnerable after finishing Barry and this pressure of "What are you going to do next" — it was all self-imposed — that I really lost my confidence. And so I set it aside.
**Bill:** 我在《巴里》结束后写了一部恐怖片。一个很大的制片人说"这太恶毒了，太可怕了。"我说"是啊，这是恐怖片。"因为刚做完《巴里》很脆弱，"接下来做什么"的压力——全是自己给自己的——我真的失去了信心。所以就搁置了。

**Bill:** But when I saw Eddington, I was like, "Yes, okay. I'm going to go back to that project because he did it. He had the balls to do the thing that I kind of was talked out of."
**Bill:** 但看了 Eddington 之后，我想"好，我要回去做那个项目了，因为他做了。他有胆量去做我被说服放弃的事情。"

**Ari:** I think the minute I started thinking strategically, I just lose steam. I told a filmmaker friend the idea and he was like, "You're just going to walk into that meat grinder?"
**Ari:** 我开始策略性思考的那一刻就失去了动力。我跟一个导演朋友说了这个想法，他说"你就这么要走进那台绞肉机？"

**Bill:** I do think it's a film that will last forever. I haven't had an experience like that in a while, where we were all in the parking lot afterwards going, "What the fuck did we just... **Bill:** 我觉得这部电影会永远留下来。好久没有那种体验了——我们在停车场面面相觑"我们刚才看了什么... 太厉害了。"Guillermo 发短信给我说"太厉害了。这是杰作。太有种了。Ari Aster 太有种了。"
